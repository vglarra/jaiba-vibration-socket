# TPU Hex Socket: Piezo Pad Vibration Damper

A parametric Blender script that generates a hexagonal TPU socket. It is glued over each hexagonal sensor pad of a piezo percussion tray, holds a round piezo sensor flush with the top, and isolates the pad from its neighbors. One socket is printed per pad (16 in total).

The tray this fits is generated by a separate Blender script (`flat_hex_plate-Walls2`). Default dimensions were taken from that script so the two parts mate.

> **Status:** the script builds a watertight mesh in Blender 5.0 (0 non-manifold edges, 0 self-intersections, volume matches the analytic value, STL export works). **Nothing has been printed yet.** Fit, stiffness and damping are untested.

## Geometry

All hexes are pointy-top (flats facing ±X, corners at 30° + 60°·i), like the tray. Hex sizes are flat-to-flat.

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Pad (tray inner platform) | 78 mm, 5 mm tall |
| Socket outer / interior | 83.4 mm / 78 mm (2.7 mm wall) |
| Overall height | 26 mm (matches the tray walls) |
| z 0 to 5 | Open underside recess that slips over the pad |
| z 5 to 8 | 3 mm floor slab |
| z 8 to 16 | Support zone: honeycomb + solid bands |
| z 16 to 26 | Sensor pocket (67.5 mm sensor + 0.2 mm clearance per side, 10 mm deep, top flush at 26) |
| Sensor ring | 2 mm wall around the pocket, z 8 to 26 |
| Moat | Open gap between ring and hex wall: 3.05 mm at the flats, about 9 mm at the corners |
| Honeycomb | 8 mm open cells, 2 mm walls, open at the top; the sensor rests on the wall tops |
| Wire hole | 12 mm through the floor, 28 mm from center toward the left flat (angle 180°), as in the tray |
| Wire bore | 10 mm through the honeycomb above the floor |

### Why 83.4 mm outside

The tray's pads are 78 mm on an 84 mm pitch, so neighboring pads are 6 mm apart. Two 2.7 mm walls take 5.4 mm of that gap and leave 0.6 mm of slack (the original 3 mm walls filled it exactly). The interior stays 78 mm: it comes from `PAD_FLAT`, and the outside is `PAD_FLAT + 2 × WALL_T`. Adding fit clearance makes neighbors overlap by 2 × the clearance minus the slack.

## Usage

1. Open Blender (tested headless on 5.0.1).
2. Scripting workspace: load `jaiba-vibration-socket.py` and run it.
3. The console prints derived dimensions, cell count, and mesh checks (non-manifold edges, volume versus expected).
4. `tpu_hex_socket.stl` is written next to the `.blend`, or to your home folder if the file is unsaved.
5. A wireframe `SensorProxy` puck shows the sensor position in the viewport (not exported).

Re-running deletes and rebuilds `TPU_Socket`.

## Cushion mode (no honeycomb)

`HONEYCOMB = False` (the default now) removes the honeycomb sensor support. The sensor ring stays, and the pocket inside it gets a flat floor at the top of the floor slab (z = 8), where the cookie cushion is glued in.

- **Pocket:** 68.0 mm across (`SENSOR_D` 67.5 + 2 × `SENSOR_CLEARANCE` 0.25), 18 mm deep (floor at z = 8, top at z = 26). The 64 × 17 cushion leaves 1 mm to spare; there is no room above it for a 10 mm sensor, so the stack height needs a decision (`TOTAL_H`, `FLOOR_T`).
- **Wire hole:** 10 mm through the floor at the same position as before. A 12 mm hole at 28 mm offset would touch the 68 mm pocket wall, so `WIRE_HOLE_DIAMETER` is 10. The cushion's 10 mm wiring notch reaches 22 to 32 mm from the centre, so it lines up with the hole when you rotate the cushion to put the notch over it.
- `CUSHION_D` and `CUSHION_H` only drive the viewport proxy and the fit printout.
- `HONEYCOMB = True` brings the old design back; the floor hole must then be wider than `WIRE_BORE_DIAMETER`.

## Hex wall above the floor

`WALL_UP` sets how tall the hex wall is above the floor slab. `0` (default now) removes it: the 2 mm outer wall stops at the floor (z = 7) and only the sensor ring rises to 26 mm. A small value, e.g. `4`, keeps a low lip as a finger guard; `19` (= `TOTAL_H` minus the floor top) restores the full wall. The 5 mm underside recess and its 78 mm interior are not affected.

## Coupon mode: test cell designs before printing a socket

Set `COUPON = True` at the top of the script to build small honeycomb test patches instead of the full socket. Set it back to `False` for the real part.

- A coupon is a hexagonal patch: 3 mm floor + open-top cells, same 8 mm cell height as the socket. Default is 7 cells, about 32 to 35 mm across, 11 mm tall, a few minutes to print.
- One coupon is built and exported per entry in `COUPON_VARIANTS`. Keys: `style`, `cell`, `wall`, `shift`, `rings`. Anything left out uses the global value.
- File names carry the settings, e.g. `coupon_accordion_c10_w1p2_s3.stl` (style, cell width, wall, shift; `p` = decimal point). Coupons are laid out side by side in the viewport.
- `COUPON_RINGS = 2` gives 19 cells for a more realistic feel. The 7-cell coupon has extra solid in its corners, so it reads a bit stiffer than a large honeycomb.
- Print all variants with identical slicer settings, then squeeze each by hand.

## Cell styles

`CELL_STYLE` applies to both the socket and the coupons.

| Style | Walls | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `'straight'` | Vertical | Original design. Short thick walls mostly squash, so it feels firm. |
| `'accordion'` | Zig-zag `<` in side view | Whole honeycomb shifts `ACCORDION_SHIFT` mm along +X at mid height and returns at the top, so walls bend instead of squashing. Constant wall thickness, top and bottom outlines align. |

Defaults: shift 3 mm over two 4 mm segments, which leans the walls 37° from vertical (the script warns above 45°). `ACCORDION_SEGMENTS` must be even. In the socket, cells that would touch the ring or wire bore in the shifted position are dropped, so accordion keeps fewer cells.

Solid fraction inside the pocket (socket, whole-cell rule): the solid band between the outer cells and the ring dominates, and it depends on cell size, not wall thickness.

| Cell width | Straight | Accordion |
|---|---|---|
| 8 mm | 55% | 61% |
| 10 mm | 55% | 55% |
| 12 mm | 36% | 46% |
| 14 mm | 70% | 70% |

Cell widths are not monotonic because only whole cells fit. 12 mm is the best of these.

## Design notes

- **No booleans.** The whole mesh, including the wire hole, is built directly, and holes are tessellated by Blender. It is watertight by construction.
- **Whole cells only.** Cells that would come within `FILL_WALL_T` of the ring or wire bore are dropped, leaving a solid band instead of ragged partial cells. Result: 28 cells and about 55% solid inside the pocket, not the roughly 36% a pure honeycomb would give. Use a larger `CELL_INNER` for a softer feel.
- **Wire bore is 10 mm, not 12.** A 12 mm bore at 28 mm offset reaches r = 34.0 against a pocket wall at r = 33.95, which leaves a 0.05 mm sliver. The 12 mm hole through the floor is unchanged. A 1 mm ledge sits between the two, over the hole.
- **The ring is attached only through the floor and honeycomb.** It is not tied to the hex wall, so the moat isolates the sensor from the neighbors. Optional bridging ribs are not implemented.
- **Open-top cells, no skin.** This avoids a 68 mm TPU bridge. The cells are sealed at the bottom by the floor, so silicone could be poured in later.

## Break-away support grid

The 78 mm recess ceiling is a large bridge in TPU. Instead of relying on slicer supports, the script can add a thin rib grid inside the recess:

- It stands on the print bed (z 0 to 4.8) and stops `SUPPORT_GAP` (0.2 mm) short of the roof, and 0.4 mm short of the recess wall. It is a **separate shell in the same STL**, so it never fuses to the socket.
- The roof prints over a grid of about 6.8 mm square cells, each supported on all four sides, instead of one 78 mm span.
- After printing, the grid lifts out in one piece. If the roof bonds to the rib tops, a small increase in `SUPPORT_GAP` helps.
- Turn off the slicer's own supports for the recess when using this. Slicers usually ask whether a two-shell STL is a multi-part object; keep it as one object.
- Set `SUPPORT_RIBS = False` to export the socket alone (for example when comparing with slicer supports).
- The grid is untested on a printer. It sits under the wire hole too, which is harmless because it is removed afterwards.

## Known issues and printing notes

- **Bridging.** The 78 mm underside recess ceiling is handled by the break-away grid above, printed cup-up. Untested.
- **Ring base.** A small fillet at the ring base may help avoid cracking in TPU. Not added.
- **Untested.** Sensor fit, stiffness, crosstalk, print settings and glue are unverified.

## Cookie cushion (`cookie-cushion.py`, v2)

A 40 mm disc printed standing on its edge, with the cushion modelled as real geometry instead of slicer infill: optional thin face plates (`FACE_T`) joined by an X-braced diamond lattice, `ROWS` diamonds deep across the thickness. Every web is a vertical sheet, so it prints as plain perimeters. You press along Y (the disc axis). A solid foot cradles the curved underside and is cut off after printing (0.2 mm gap under the disc, 0.4 mm on the steep flanks).

- **Softness:** the struts bend under load. Wide, flat diamonds (`CELL_W` large, more `ROWS`) and thin webs (`WEB_T`) are softer. Smaller diamonds with the same web thickness are stiffer.
- **Estimate:** the script prints an estimated stiffness vs the first printed cookie (rows 1, 8 mm, web 0.9, plates 0.9), from a simplified 2-D frame model. It is relative and unmeasured; it ignores TPU damping.
- **Foot:** cradle up to `SUPPORT_ANGLE`, base plate (`FLANGE`) and two thin braces with gussets that stand 0.35 mm from each face and stop the disc swaying while it prints. Set `BRACES = False` to drop them.
- `VARIANTS` exports one STL per entry (`cookie_r2_cw8_web0p6_f0p45.stl` = rows 2, cell width 8, web 0.6, plates 0.45). `face=0` gives open faces (not exported by default).
- **Wiring notch:** `WIRE_HOLE = True` cuts a half-stadium slot through the whole thickness, open to the rim: `WIRE_D` wide (10 mm), reaching `WIRE_DEPTH` (10 mm) in from the rim, at `WIRE_ANGLE` degrees from the top. Keep it within about 40 degrees of the top; a side notch would have to bridge its roof.
- Thicker discs with the same `ROWS` have steeper struts and are stiffer: at 17 mm thick, 3 rows gives about the softness that 2 rows gave at 10 mm. Check the printed estimate.
- File names include the size (`cookie_d64x17_r2_cw8_...`).
- Needs Blender 4.2+ (exact boolean). Tested headless on 5.0.1.
- Slicer (checked by slicing in SuperSlicer 2.5.59.13): perimeters 2, infill 100%, top and bottom solid layers 0, thin walls on, gap fill on, no supports, brim on. Webs of 0.45 mm print as single thin-wall lines, 0.6 mm as outlines, 0.9 mm as loops plus gap fill. Keep it as one object.
- Print and squeeze to compare; nothing here is measured.
